Where Dawn lives

Five places the blue could earn a job. Ranked by whether it’s saying something or just being pretty.

The constraint that shapes all of these. Dawn on white measures 2.46:1 — too low for text, and too low for a bare shape. But Ink on solid Dawn is 7.35:1, and ink on a 20% Dawn tint is 15.4:1. So Dawn works beautifully as a surface with dark text on it, and never as ink itself. Every option below respects that. Where a Dawn-colored word was wanted, it's deepened to #4c63b6 (5.57:1).

The home chip — what color is the word?

If you want white text, take the deepened Dawn #4c63b6. It is unmistakably the same blue — just carrying enough weight to hold white on it. The trade is honest: the chip is then a relative of Dawn rather than Dawn itself, so the palette gains a seventh color. Ink on true Dawn is the alternative that adds nothing and keeps the swatch pure, but it reads softer and more pastel than what you described.

If you want one

You picked 2 — the home chip

Option 1 gives Dawn a meaning rather than a location, which is how the rest of this palette already works — Klein acts, Noon and the moon report, Amber exists only to make yellow legal. “Dawn means you’re not looking at live time” fits that logic, and it solves a real confusion: nothing else on screen currently distinguishes a simulated March from right now.

Option 3 pairs with it cleanly because the two never collide — one is a banner at the top, the other a small tag on a row, and both are exceptions rather than furniture. Together Dawn shows up perhaps twice on a busy screen and not at all on a quiet one, which is what “minor accent” should mean.
