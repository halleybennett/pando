01Color

A Sky gradient carries the mood; white cards float on top of it. Ink does all the reading. Klein blue is the only color that acts. Dawn and Noon are pure data: they encode where a city is in its day, and are never decoration.

The rule, added 4 Sept 2026. Every UI color — the add button, the sun, the moon, the home chip — must be a color the time-picker wheel actually paints. Not "from the palette", not "near it": on the wheel. In dark mode they are not chosen at all, they are sampled from it with the same treatment the wheel uses, so they cannot drift. Three earlier candidates died on this rule — Posie, an amber chip, and a lifted Klein — each a color that existed nowhere else on screen.

One exemption, deliberate: the sun's Amber outline. Noon on a white card measures 1.57 and the disc vanishes; no wheel stop can do that job. Recorded here rather than quietly allowed.

The dial ramp

Keyed to the hour, not symmetric around noon. An even ramp either side of midday hands half the circle to blue and makes the day look short. This one is skewed so cool runs only 21:00–06:30 — the same window as the sun/moon threshold, so the ring and the city rows say the same thing.

Painted, not declared. It is 144 conic segments of SVG stroke, because a CSS gradient cannot express an asymmetric hour mapping. The arc on the dial is drawn from the same function, so arc and ring always agree.

Soft  — live
Full  — held in reserve
midnightdawnnoonduskmidnight
Two strengths, one shape. Soft is live. Full is the saturated original, kept so the decision can be reversed without redoing the work. They share identical timing — only intensity differs, so swapping between them changes how loud the dial is and nothing else. In code this is a single constant: --ramp-soften: 0.46, the fraction each stop is mixed toward white. Set it to 0 for full. It moved out of app.js and into the stylesheet on 4 Sept 2026, when every colour in the dial was tokenised so a second scheme could exist.

How to read that. Going full does not fix the weak end. The day band only moves 1.28 → 1.57, because the ramp's gold and the dial's white face are both very light — no amount of saturation separates them. What full actually buys is the night end, 3.38 → 11.81, and Noon keeping all 0.150 of its chroma instead of being washed to 0.092. So “brighter” means a harder blue and a truer gold, not a more legible clock.

The third option, and why it is not on the table for light. Remapping lightness in OKLab — the treatment that fixed dark mode — does finally move the day band, to 2.30. But to darken gold enough to separate from white it has to take Noon to #d2a509, which is mustard. It buys legibility by giving up the colour, and Noon is the one colour on the dial that is carrying a meaning. Rejected.

Hour ticks — darkened 4 Sept 2026

The major tick moved #8d8792 → #79717e. At the old value it measured exactly 3.00 against every paper tried — dead on the 3:1 floor a meaningful graphic needs, with no margin anywhere. It had quietly blocked three rounds of ground work, because any deeper ground pushed it under. It now scores 4.05 at worst across the Sky gradient, and stays lighter than the numerals so the hierarchy on the rim survives.

The minor tick moved #c2bcc6 → #aea7b3, only to hold its old relationship to the majors. These are half-hour subdivisions, redundant with both the hour ticks and the numerals — decorative, so the 3:1 rule does not reach them. They measure 2.02 and are not pretending otherwise. Darkening them to clear 3:1 would flatten the rim into one undifferentiated ring of lines.

The chip and the sun are the same colour, and that is deliberate. Opposite ends of the same row — a labelled pill on the left, a rayed disc on the right. Different objects, one colour. Opacity is not a legibility tool here. The already-added row was faded to 40%, which put its label at 1.75. Raising the opacity cannot rescue it — even 85% only reaches 3.90, because it is fading a colour that starts at 5.39 on white. It is now quietened by dropping the city name from Ink to Muted instead: the row still reads as unavailable, and every part of it clears 4.5.

Name in Body 300, zone line in Label, time in Display 200 with tabular numerals. Rows divide with hairlines, not borders. Max 6 rows, then the add row disappears entirely. One line per row, permanently — the country sits inline beside the city name, not on a second line. A fold-on-drag alternative was prototyped and rejected as distracting; the list never animates. Tap a row for its actions: Set home and Delete, both ≥44pt. Swipe left opens the same panel.

Home chip. Solid Dawn #8ba2f0 pill, Ink text at 12.5px uppercase, letter-spacing:.13em, 3px / 10px padding. Marks the one city the dial speaks for; tapping another row moves it. Never white text — white on Dawn is 2.46:1, the same ratio that keeps Dawn out of type everywhere else.

Day-state marker — locked

 Asleep — local time 21:00–06:59
 Awake — local time 07:00–20:59

Binary, and every row carries one. Three states were tested and dropped: without a contrasting mark beside it, a lone symbol doesn't explain itself. A mark only means something next to a mark that differs — so both states are always drawn.

Recipe. Moon: solid Klein #0d21a5, no outline needed at 11.81:1. Sun: Noon #f7c948 disc with a 1px Amber #a97103 outline and rays, 4.16:1. Both 18×18 on a 24px hit area. Yellow is never drawn bare.

Passes WCAG 1.4.1. The two glyphs differ in silhouette, not just hue — a crescent and a rayed disc survive having their color removed entirely. Color is reinforcement here, not the message.

The dial — locked

DecisionValue
FormFull centred 24-hour wheel. Midnight top, noon bottom.
ArcOuter — rides between the ramp band and the hour ticks.
RampSoft (see §01). Full is kept and reversible.
MarkerThe arc's own rounded cap. No notch, bar or knob.
Drag surfaceThe entire dial, middle included.
Steps5 minutes on a normal drag; 1 minute when the drag slows below ~0.75 rad/s.
Drag feedbackTicks thicken and turn Klein on entering fine mode, with a label under the dial.

Geometry (400px viewBox): white face r 104 · ramp band 104–142 · outer arc track r 155, 13px wide · ticks 168–180 · numerals r 192. The 26px gap between band and ticks is deliberate — the earlier 4px version read as strangled.

Why the marker can be this quiet. Because the whole dial is the drag target, the marker never has to be aimed at, so it is an indicator rather than a hit area and is free to be as small as it likes. Taken from the reference app, which has no handle at all — see reference/NOTES.md.

Known accepted gap — no keyboard or VoiceOver path. A drag-only control cannot be operated by keyboard, switch control or VoiceOver, so the dial is unusable for those users and the app fails WCAG 2.1.1 (Keyboard). This is a deliberate decision, not an oversight: accepted 3 Sept 2026 for the current scope. The fix is one typed time input beside the readout. Revisit before any public release.

05Icons & imagery

No icon library, no photography, no illustration. At this size a font-icon set would be more bytes than the whole app.

Every glyph is hand-drawn inline SVG on a 24×24 grid: 1.25px stroke, round caps, no fill, currentColor. The set is four — search, plus, chevron, close — plus the sun and moon in §04. The dial is SVG on the same principles, and its tick marks are generated, not drawn.

The mark

64px
24px
16px · in use

Three stalks of uneven height, each carrying a leaf, rising from a broken soil line. One root system showing above ground as many trees — which is what a pando is. Solid blades on 0.95 stalks; the soil line is three dashes at 1.05 and 60% opacity, which the stalks do not touch.

Always 16px, never 12. The mark is sized independently of the 12px label beside it — a brand mark has no obligation to match the type it sits next to. The soil dashes are the finest detail in the system and the first thing to vanish; at 12px they fall below a device pixel on a 1× screen.

Never join the stalks with a solid bar. A continuous base under three uprights is a candle holder at any size. The rejected alternatives are worth recording, because each one solved the candelabra by becoming a different wrong object: converging stalks make an anchor, a forked trunk makes a clover, removing the stalks makes falling rain. Asymmetry does the real work — uneven spacing and uneven leaf sizes are why three uprights read as a stand of trees rather than as an object.
