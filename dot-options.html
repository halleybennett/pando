How few marks?

You liked B (fill state) and C (sun & moon). Here’s the same idea at four levels of simplification — from three symbols down to none.

Every option is shown twice — in color, then with color stripped out. The gray version is the test: if the three states are still distinguishable there, it works for people who can’t separate the hues, and in bright sun on a bad screen.

The earlier six-option sheet (A–F: color only, fill state, sun & moon, mini dial, words, day bar) is superseded by this page — B and C are the only two carried forward.

Decided 3 Sept 2026 — Level 2, flavor C. Sun and moon, binary, a mark on every row. Level 1 was rejected for a good reason: without a contrasting mark beside it, a lone symbol doesn’t explain itself. The rest of this page is kept as the record of what was considered.

The ladder

Where I’d land

Level 1 — one symbol, on the night rows only

The app answers exactly one question: is this a bad moment to contact them? For most cities most of the time, the answer is no — so there is nothing worth marking. Marking the exception rather than the state means a screen with six cities usually carries one or two marks total, not six.

It also survives the gray test on the strongest channel available: presence versus absence. There's no pair of similar shapes to tell apart, because there's only ever one shape.

And it's where C keeps its charm without the cost — a single moon against five unmarked rows is a nice touch. Five suns in a column was the thing that made C feel like a weather app, and at this level that can't happen.

What you give up: the dawn/dusk middle state. You'd no longer see "they're up, but only just." If that distinction matters to you, Level 2 keeps it cheaply — but I'd argue "asleep or not" is the real question and the rest is decoration.
