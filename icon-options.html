Six grounds, every colour taken from the shipped palette or the wheel. The mark is the locked one, rendered live — the sliders retune all six at once.

One thing is fixed in all six, and it is a real bug rather than a preference. In the 16px mark the stalks stop 0.8 units above the soil dashes — but the stalk's rounded cap is 0.475 deep and the soil stroke is 0.525, so they overlap by 0.2. At 16px that is a fifth of a device pixel and reads as the gap it is meant to be. At 512px it is six visible pixels of the stalks touching the ground, which is the one thing this mark is not supposed to do. The gap is now derived from the two stroke weights, so it holds at any size.

The soil line is still an open question — it is the toggle above. At icon scale the dashes stop reading as a line and start reading as three separate objects, which is why unified and none are on the table alongside the faithful version.

How the numbers work. The figure under each is the mark against the worst point of its ground — for a gradient that is the stop it contrasts least with, not the average. An icon is a graphic, so 3:1 is the floor; anything below is marked red and is out regardless of how it looks.
