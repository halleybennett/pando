The mark

A real aspen leaf, built parametrically rather than drawn — so the shape, the taper of the veins and the fineness of the line are all knobs rather than commitments. Every treatment is shown at icon sizes and at the 16px it has to survive in the header.

What the reference is actually doing. The veins are tapered — fat where they leave the base, drawn to a fine point at the margin — and they fan from a single origin rather than branching off a midrib. That taper is most of the quality, and it is why a uniform-width stroke never looks like it. Here the veins are built as filled spindles, so they can carry the same weight variation.

Why the old mark read as childish. A teardrop on a stick is a generic silhouette — it belongs equally to a candle, a raindrop and a balloon, which is exactly the confusion you named. An aspen leaf is not generic: broadly ovate, abruptly pointed, palmately veined from the base, on a long slender stalk. The specificity is the sophistication.

The 16px row is the real test. Fine veins will disappear there no matter how good they look at 512. If nothing survives, the honest answer is two marks — a detailed one for the icon and a reduced one for the header, which is ordinary practice — and that row will tell us.
