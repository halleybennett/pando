I was wrong about the bright wheel in light mode, and it is worth saying so. Last round I reported that lifting in OKLab turns Noon to mustard. That was true of the version I had tuned to force the day band away from white — floor 0.30, ceiling 0.80. At the same settings dark mode uses, Noon lands at #f3c543, which is Noon. So the bright wheel is one treatment across both schemes, and it beats the soft wheel on the numbers in light: night band 3.38 → 4.87, day band 1.28 → 1.63, chroma 0.092 → 0.150. It is louder. That is the whole question, and it is a taste one.

0.70 works in dark. In light it misses, by 0.08. On the dark face 0.70 puts Sky's night band at 4.36 — comfortable. On the white face the same setting gives 2.92, just under the 3:1 a meaningful graphic needs. The crossover is at 0.68 (3.10), so light is set there and dark to your 0.70. Two clicks apart on the slider and not a difference you can see — but 0.70 in light would have been a number that quietly fails, which is the sort of thing that only shows up in an audit six months later.

The chip is the wheel's gold, and the wheel offers exactly one yellow. Its three warm stops — cream at 09:30, noon gold, warming-down at 19:30 — measure 1.01, 1.06 and 1.07 apart from each other once lifted. That is one colour with three names. So there is no toggle here: a yellow chip is the yellow, #f9cb4a, sampled from the noon plateau.

Which means the chip and the sun are the same colour, and that is the thing to look at. They sit at opposite ends of the same row — a labelled pill on the left, a rayed disc on the right. Different objects, one colour. The precedent is already in light mode, where the add button and the moon are both Klein and nobody has ever misread them.

The one thing worth watching: when home is awake the row shows a gold chip and a gold sun; when it is asleep, a gold chip and a blue moon. So the chip matches the day-state marker half the time. Light mode already does this in mirror image — a Dawn chip beside a Klein moon — so it is not new, but it is easier to see in gold.

Against the add button it is the best separation available anywhere in the wheel: #f9cb4a against #6194ff is gold against blue, the full width of the ramp. That was the collision you flagged, and it is now as far from happening as this palette can get.
